Anatomy of a Second

A 40 mm dive watch holds 186 parts, 25 jewels and a balance wheel that swings 28,800 times an hour. Scroll and it comes apart layer by layer, in the order a watchmaker opens it.

  • Height12.4 mm
  • Water300 m

I

Step I · The case

Milled from a single block of steel

The middle case is cut from 904L stainless steel, brushed on top of the lugs and polished along the flanks. First the bracelet comes off: two spring bars, pressed in with a tweezer tip.

904L stainless steel

II

Step II · Crystal & bezel

Sapphire above, ceramic around

The crystal is synthetic sapphire, 9 on the Mohs scale, with anti-reflective coating on the underside only. The bezel turns one way in 120 clicks: if it gets knocked, a dive can only look longer than it was, never shorter.

Crystal
Sapphire, 2.5 mm, domed
Bezel
Ceramic insert, 120 clicks
Pip
Luminous, at 60 minutes

III

Step III · Dial & hands

Twelve applied indices

Each index is a separate piece of white gold, filled with luminous pigment and riveted through the dial. The seconds hand doesn't glide. It steps eight times a second, once for every beat of the balance.

Dial
Sunburst blue lacquer
Hands
Hour, minute, seconds
Seconds
8 steps per second

IV

Step IV · Caseback

A window onto the movement

Turn the watch over. The caseback screws into the case with a special wrench, and a sapphire disc in its centre lets you watch the movement run without opening anything.

Fitting
Screw-down

V

Step V · Winding rotor

Wound by the wrist

The half-moon rotor sits on a ball bearing and swings with every movement of your arm. Through a reverser wheel it winds the mainspring whichever way it turns. The heavy rim at its edge gives it the momentum.

Winding
Bidirectional
Bearing
Ceramic balls
Finish
Côtes de Genève

VI

Step VI · The going train

Bridges, wheels and a coiled spring

Under the bridges sits the going train. The mainspring in the barrel stores 70 hours of energy and lets it out through the centre wheel, which turns once an hour and carries the minute hand, then the third wheel and the fourth, which turns once a minute and carries the seconds.

Barrel
1 turn per 8 h
Centre wheel
1 turn per hour
Fourth wheel
1 turn per minute
Plate
Perlage, rhodium-plated

VII

Step VII · Escapement & balance

Where time is cut into beats

The escape wheel pushes the pallet fork, the fork kicks the balance, and the hairspring pulls it back. Each swing frees the escape wheel by half a tooth. The balance runs at 4 Hz; here it is slowed to one-eighth so you can follow it.
